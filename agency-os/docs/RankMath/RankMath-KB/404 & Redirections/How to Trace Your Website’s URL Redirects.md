# How to Trace Your Website’s URL Redirects

			
			

	

Have you ever clicked a link expecting to land on a specific page but ended up somewhere completely different? This can be frustrating for both you and your website visitors. It often occurs due to URL redirects.

While redirects are a common practice, tracing where your URLs or links actually lead is crucial.

In this knowledgebase article, we will walk you through various methods to easily trace redirects, guaranteeing that you, your visitors, and search engines always reach the intended destination.

## How to Trace Redirects

There are several ways to trace redirects on your website. Here, we’ll explore two user-friendly methods:

### 1  Using a WordPress Plugin (Rank Math SEO)

If you’re already using the [Rank Math SEO plugin](https://wordpress.org/plugins/seo-by-rank-math/) to manage your website’s redirections, you can leverage its built-in debugging feature to trace them easily. Here’s how:

Log in to your WordPress dashboard and navigate to **Rank Math SEO → Redirections.**Next, click the **Settings**button at the top of the Redirections page.

![Rank Math Redirections page](https://rankmath.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Redirections-page.png)

This will take you to the Redirections settings tab. Under the Redirections tab, find the **Debug Redirections** option and enable it, as shown below. After that, click the **Save Changes** button to save your settings.

![Setting up Debug Redirections in Rank Math](https://rankmath.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Redirections-settings.png)

Once the settings are saved, you can now open any redirections you created using Rank Math. When you open these redirections, you will see that Rank Math’s debug mode will display an interstitial page.

This page will show you details about the redirect, including the source URL, destination URL, and management options. With this information, you can confirm and manage the redirect path if needed.

![Rank Math Redirect Debugger ](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201371%20616'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

The interstitial page disappears after 5 seconds and is only visible to website administrators. You can temporarily stop the redirection by clicking the **Stop Redirection** option below the count.

Additionally, you can manage the redirection by clicking the **Manage This Redirection** link.

![Rank Math Interstitial page to trace Redirects](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201143%20549'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

Clicking on **Manage This Redirection** will direct you to the specific redirect rule causing the redirection, enabling you to diagnose and fix unwanted redirections.

![Add or Edit redirections in Rank Math](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201260%20795'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

When you are done with your adjustments, you can save your changes.

Please remember that this method is specifically for tracing redirects set up using the Rank Math SEO plugin. If you do not have Rank Math installed on your WordPress website, [consider installing it](https://rankmath.com/kb/installing-through-the-admin-panel/)to manage all SEO aspects effectively.

### 2  Using an Online Redirect Checker

If the redirects on your website were not set up by Rank Math, but instead by other plugins or server-side redirects, you can use an online redirect checker like httpstatus.io to trace them.

This tool provides a detailed analysis of the [redirect chain](https://rankmath.com/seo-glossary/redirect-chain/) for any URL, helping you understand how your redirects are functioning.

To begin, visit [httpstatus.io](http://httpstatus.io) using your web browser. In the input field on the homepage, enter the URL you want to trace and click the **Check status** button.

![httpstatus.io homepage to insert URL](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201399%20647'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

Note that you can enter multiple URLs to trace their redirects, but you will want to add them per line. When you click the button, the tool will analyze the URLs and display a report if any redirects are present.

The report will typically show the number of redirects, the final destination URL, and the redirect status codes.

![httpstatus.io analyzes URLs for redirects](data:image/svg+xml,%3Csvg%20xmlns='http://www.w3.org/2000/svg'%20viewBox='0%200%201399%20664'%3E%3C/svg%3E)

You can examine the redirect path to identify any unnecessary or problematic redirects. Based on the analysis, you can make the necessary adjustments to your redirects to optimize your site’s performance and user experience.

By following these steps, you can effectively trace and manage your URL redirects, ensuring your website remains user-friendly and optimized for search engines.
