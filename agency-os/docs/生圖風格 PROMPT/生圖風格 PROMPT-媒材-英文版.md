## 1. Watercolor｜Transparent Wash and Soft Bleeding Style

### Style Core

The core of watercolor is transparency, fluidity, lightness, bleeding, and the absorbent quality of paper. It is not a heavy, opaque painting method; instead, pigment and water spread, dissolve, overlap, and settle naturally on the paper surface, creating a soft and breathable image. This style is suitable for morning light, mist, flowers, figures, natural landscapes, travel sketches, poetic scenes, and quiet everyday moments.

For image generation, the focus is not merely making the picture pale. The key is to show water, paper, transparent layers, and softened edges. The subject may remain clear, but it should not become overly sharp. The background may be left blank, softened, or allowed to diffuse naturally so that the image retains airiness and a handmade feeling.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should show obvious watercolor paper texture, including paper fibers, grain, water absorption marks, tide lines, and pigment sediment. Color should not look like a perfectly smooth digital gradient. It should contain natural variations in density, soft bleeding edges, localized water stains, and traces of pigment settling into the paper. Transparent layers may overlap one another, with darker areas built through repeated glazing rather than a single heavy application of black.

The subject’s edges should combine both hard edges and soft edges. Areas of focus, such as eyes, petals, architectural outlines, or object edges, may be slightly clearer. Areas outside the focal point should naturally dissolve, fade, or bleed into the background. Backgrounds often contain generous blank space, pale color haze, or flowing color fields, and do not need to fill the entire image.

Brushwork should feel light, transparent, and water-rich. Wet-on-wet techniques may create soft misty bleeding, while dry-brush marks may break across the paper grain. Watercolor is particularly effective at depicting light and air. The image should feel translucent, gentle, and slightly unfinished, rather than overly polished, overly sharp, or plastically smooth. Overall, it should feel as if pigment is being carried by water, with chance, breath, and handmade warmth.

### Color Vocabulary

Main colors include pale blue, gray-blue, soft pink, light purple, tender green, ivory white, tea brown, light ocher, and soft yellow. Colors should be transparent, low to medium in saturation, and lightened through dilution with water.

Shadows should not use dead black. They can be built with blue-gray, violet-gray, tea brown, or diluted complementary colors. Highlights are usually preserved by leaving the paper white rather than covering them with thick white paint. The overall palette should feel fresh, gentle, and translucent.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

## 2. Oil Painting｜Thick Painterly Classical Oil Style

### Style Core

The core of oil painting is thickness, layering, light and shadow, brushwork, pigment texture, and a sculptural sense of volume. It can be classical, realistic, Baroque, Impressionist, or modern expressionist, but its shared characteristic is the physical weight of paint. Oil paint can build depth through glazing, impasto, scraping, and visible brushstrokes.

For image generation, the focus should not be simply making the image look photographic. The key is to show the oil-painted surface, brushstroke direction, pigment thickness, and light-and-shadow modeling. The subject should have the warm density of oil paint. Shadows may be deep, highlights may be thickly painted, and the entire image should look as though real pigment has been layered on canvas.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should show visible canvas texture and layered oil paint. Dark areas may be deep and transparent, as if formed through multiple glazed layers. Highlights may have impasto thickness, with slightly raised paint texture. Brushstroke direction should serve the form: facial strokes should follow the turns of muscles, fabric strokes should follow folds, and sky or background strokes may be looser.

Oil painting can have strong lighting or soft lighting, but it should always convey pigment thickness and plastic volume. Figures, still lifes, landscapes, architecture, or abstract subjects can all be given weight through oil paint. The image should not look like smooth digital airbrushing. It should show bristle marks, pigment buildup, color mixing, and localized scraping.

Composition may be stable, dramatic, or free, but the surface must feel genuinely painted. Edges can vary between sharp and softened; focal areas may be more detailed, while backgrounds can remain loose. Color blending should feel natural to oil paint rather than mechanically smooth. Overall, the image should be dense, warm, deep, and rich with painterly presence.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include ocher, burnt umber, deep red, ultramarine, olive green, warm white, gray-blue, earth yellow, and black-brown. Colors can be rich, heavy, low-saturation, or high-contrast depending on the subject.

Highlights may use warm white, golden yellow, or pale flesh tones. Shadows may use burnt umber, deep blue, or black-brown. The overall palette should have the density and depth of mixed oil pigments rather than flat printed color.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Traditional oil painting style, visible canvas texture and oil pigment thickness, clear painterly brushstrokes, deep transparent shadows, impasto highlights in the bright areas, subject modeled with oil-paint light and shadow to create volume and weight, layered color buildup, edges varying between sharp and softened, ocher, burnt umber, deep red, ultramarine, olive green, warm white, and gray-blue color palette, overall dense, warm, classical, and handmade, not using smooth digital airbrush effects, but building the image through oil brushwork, pigment buildup, and canvas texture.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid glossy CGI, 3D render, plastic texture, no brushstrokes, no canvas texture, smooth digital illustration, vector graphics, anime style, overly sharp photography, neon sci-fi, low texture, flat poster look, overly clean finish, cheap filter effect, no pigment thickness.

## 3. Acrylic｜Bold Opaque Modern Paint Style

### Style Core

The core of acrylic painting is brightness, clarity, strong opacity, modernity, and a fast-drying painted surface. It is more opaque than watercolor and drier, cleaner, and more immediate than oil paint. It can create flat color blocks, hard-edge shapes, abstract compositions, decorative patterns, while also preserving brush marks and palette-knife marks.

For image generation, the focus is opaque paint, vivid color blocks, clear edges, and a modern painted surface. The image can be bright, lively, graphic, or rough, but it should always show handmade pigment traces.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should show the covering power of acrylic paint. Color blocks can be clean, saturated, and sharply defined. They should not look like transparent watercolor bleeding or deep oil glazing. After drying, the surface may appear matte or semi-gloss, with brush marks, roller marks, palette-knife marks, localized thick paint, and traces of layered coverage.

The composition may lean modern, suitable for large flat areas, geometric blocks, abstract shapes, stylized figures, decorative backgrounds, clear outlines, and high-contrast colors. If the subject is representational, forms should be simplified into clear color planes rather than rendered like a photograph. Focal areas can be strengthened with bold outlines or thick painted edges.

Acrylic can be clean or rough, but it should not look overly transparent or soft-focus. The image should feel direct, bright, and intentionally controlled. Layers can be built through overlapping color blocks, exposed underlayers, dry-brush texture, and opaque coverage. Overall, the effect should be modern, clear, vivid, and physically painted.

### Color Vocabulary

Useful colors include bright red, cobalt blue, emerald green, bright yellow, orange, purple, black, and white. Earth tones and grays can be added for a modern balance. Colors are usually more saturated than watercolor and cleaner than oil paint.

High-contrast blocks, complementary colors, flat backgrounds, and strong outlines are well suited to this style. Avoid overly muddy or low-saturation colors unless a minimalist or industrial effect is intended.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Acrylic paint style, opaque high-coverage color blocks, vivid and clean flat painted surface, clear edges, visible brush marks, palette-knife marks, and layered paint texture, modern, direct, and bright composition, can include geometric blocks, simplified forms, and high-contrast colors, bright red, cobalt blue, emerald green, bright yellow, orange, purple, black, and white color palette, overall vivid, modern, dry, and handmade, not using transparent watercolor bleeding or deep oil glazing, but building the image through opaque color planes and acrylic paint texture.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, glossy CGI, 3D render, transparent watercolor look, excessive soft focus, no brush marks, no paint coverage, low-saturation darkness, plastic-looking digital illustration, delicate oil glazing, neon sci-fi, overly complex detail, no color-block structure.

## 4. Sketch｜Observational Line Drawing Style

### Style Core

The core of sketching is observation, line, structure, value, form analysis, and a sense of draftsmanship. It does not depend on decorative color. Instead, it uses lines, planes, hatching, eraser marks, blank space, and paper texture to build the image. A sketch may be a quick study or a complete tonal drawing.

For image generation, the focus is hand-drawn lines and the process of understanding form. The image should feel like an artist observing a subject with pencil, charcoal, or graphite on paper, not like a black-and-white photo filter or a clean vector outline.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should contain many hand-drawn marks: contour lines, construction lines, guide lines, hatching, cross-hatching, eraser marks, and localized corrections. Lines may vary in weight. Some can be accurate and clear, while others may feel exploratory. Focal areas should have denser and more precise linework, while non-focal areas may remain loose and lightly indicated.

Form should be built through both line and value. Shadows can be created with diagonal hatching, cross-hatching, smudged gray areas, or preserved paper highlights, rather than smooth digital gray. Proportion, skeleton, perspective, volume, and structural turning points should be visible, giving the drawing an academic study or life-drawing quality.

Paper is essential. The image should preserve off-white, gray-white, or rough paper texture. It may include unfinished edges, blank areas, draft marks, erased lines, or redrawn contours. Overall, the image should feel real, quiet, observational, and full of the friction between hand and paper, rather than a digital monochrome filter.

### Color Vocabulary

Main tones include graphite gray, pencil gray, deep gray, light gray, paper white, ivory white, charcoal black. A small amount of warm brown or aged paper tone may appear.

Color should remain restrained. The focus is line and value rather than chromatic effect. Avoid high-saturation color unless used as a tiny mark or mixed-media accent.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Traditional sketch style, strong hand-drawn linework and observational draft quality, including contour lines, construction lines, hatching, cross-hatching, eraser marks, and localized corrections, form built through line-based value and tonal structure, clear paper texture, graphite gray, deep gray, light gray, paper white, and ivory white color palette, overall quiet, focused, and resembling an artist’s study or life drawing, not using black-and-white photo filters or smooth digital linework, but building the image through real hand-drawn marks, paper blank space, and value exploration.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid black-and-white photo filter, 3D render, CGI, thick oil painting, colorful illustration, vector graphics, anime line art, overly clean finish, no paper texture, no hatching, no hand-drawn correction marks, neon colors, smooth digital shading, plastic texture.

## 5. Pencil｜Fine Graphite Tonal Drawing Style

### Style Core

The core of pencil drawing is delicacy, grayscale, controlled linework, graphite sheen, and layered tonal modeling. Compared with broad sketching, pencil emphasizes the fine marks of graphite on paper, pressure variations, soft gray transitions, and refined detail.

For image generation, the focus is graphite strokes, paper grain, delicate grayscale, and manually built light and shadow. The image may be realistic or sketch-like, but it must retain pencil linework and graphite texture.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should show fine pencil marks and the pressure variations of graphite across the paper. Shadows should be created through multiple layers of light hatching, cross-hatching, or soft blending, not through simple digital grayscale. Dark areas may have the slight sheen of deeply pressed graphite, while highlights should be preserved as paper white or lifted with eraser marks.

Contours may be clear but not rigid. Skin, hair, fabric, object surfaces, plants, or architectural details should be rendered through small hand-drawn marks. Hair can be drawn in strands, metal or glass can be described through grayscale contrast, and skin should be modeled with subtle transitions rather than airbrushed smoothness.

Paper texture should remain visible, especially in large gray areas where pencil grain and paper tooth appear. The drawing may contain unfinished edges, faint construction lines, or eraser traces. Overall, it should feel clean, delicate, quiet, and patiently handmade, not like a converted grayscale photograph.

### Color Vocabulary

Main tones include light gray, mid gray, deep gray, graphite black, paper white, and ivory white. Slight warm-gray or cool-gray variations may appear, but vivid color should not be present.

Graphite black should not become dead black. It should retain pencil layering and a slight reflective quality. Highlights should preserve the breath of the paper surface.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 6. Colored Pencil｜Layered Hand-drawn Color Pencil Style

### Style Core

The core of colored pencil is fine line texture, layered color, paper grain, and gentle but controlled color. Unlike watercolor, it does not naturally bleed. Unlike oil paint, it does not have thick coverage. Instead, it builds color depth through repeated strokes and layered colors.

For image generation, the focus is the direction of colored pencil strokes, graininess, layered color, and paper texture. The image can be refined, childlike, realistic, or illustrative, but it must preserve hand-drawn line texture.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should show small colored pencil strokes and tiny gaps of paper grain. Color should not be smooth digital fill. It should be built from multiple layers of transparent or semi-transparent pencil marks. In some areas, red, blue, yellow, green, and other colors may overlap to form mixed tones, rather than appearing as direct digital color fills.

Edges can be delicate and clear, making this style suitable for figures, animals, flowers, food, everyday objects, children’s book illustrations, and natural elements. Shadows can be gradually deepened with darker colored pencils. Highlights may preserve paper white or use pale pencils. Stroke direction should follow the form: fur should follow growth direction, petals should follow vein structure, and fabric folds should follow fold lines.

The overall texture should feel gentle, handmade, and detailed. It should not look like smooth digital illustration, nor like thick crayon blocks. Colored pencil should feel patiently layered, with some white paper grain left visible to create natural breathing space.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include soft pink, pale blue, tender green, creamy yellow, orange-red, brown, purple, gray-blue, and ivory white. The palette can be high or low in saturation, but it should show pencil layering.

Shadows can be built by layering complementary colors, such as blue-violet over yellow-orange or reddish brown over flesh tones. Avoid single dead-black shadows and fluorescent digital colors.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 7. Charcoal｜Bold Black-and-white Dramatic Value Style

### Style Core

The core of charcoal is black-and-white contrast, rough powder, strong value structure, smudging, and deep black intensity. It is darker, looser, and more dramatic than pencil, capable of quickly building large dark areas and powerful light-and-shadow effects.

For image generation, the focus is charcoal dust, paper texture, deep black, smudging, and raw handwork. The image should not be clean and precise like vector line art. It should contain powder, smears, rubbed areas, and eraser-lifted highlights.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should contain large areas of deep black and smudged gray tones. Dark regions can feel like charcoal powder accumulated on the paper, with powdery edges, dragged smears, and areas rubbed with fingers. Highlights can be lifted with an eraser, leaving broken paper texture and white scratch marks.

Lines can be thick or thin, but they should feel forceful. Portraits, figures, dramatic poses, dark interiors, strongly lit still lifes, or theatrical scenes are well suited to this medium. Contours do not need to be overly precise, but the form should feel powerful. Charcoal can create weight through large black masses and capture motion through quick gesture lines.

Paper texture is extremely important. Deep black areas should not be perfectly smooth; they should contain grain, powder, and smudging. Overall, the image should feel strong, rough, direct, and dramatically lit, as if an artist is shaping light and shadow vigorously on paper.

### Color Vocabulary

Main tones include charcoal black, deep gray, mid gray, light gray, paper white, and warm ivory. It may be monochrome or slightly warmed by the paper tone.

Black should be rich but powdery. Gray transitions should show smudging rather than digital gradients.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 8. Pastel｜Soft Powdery Color Style

### Style Core

The core of pastel is softness, mist, powdery grain, layered color dust, gentle light, and a surface that feels as if it can be blended by hand. It exists between drawing and powder, and its colors can be vivid or soft, but they usually carry a dry, matte, dusty, paper-grain quality.

For image generation, the focus is the feeling of powder attached to paper. The image should not be smooth. It should show pastel particles, rubbing marks, soft edges, and localized buildup of dry color.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should have misty edges and visible grain. Color can be blended with fingers or blending tools so that skin, sky, flowers, fabric, or background areas show gentle transitions. Certain outlines may be redrawn with pastel sticks, but the overall image should avoid overly hard edges.

Pastel is well suited for portraits, children, flowers, dream imagery, clouds, dancers, soft interiors, and light-filled scenes. The image should show dry pigment resting on paper texture. Even dark areas should not feel wet like oil paint; they should retain a matte powdery surface.

Multiple pastel layers may overlap to create rich yet soft colors. Finger blending marks, dusty edges, exposed paper grain, and slight powder-loss texture all increase authenticity. Overall, the image should feel soft, intimate, airy, and tactile, with the warmth of powdered pigment.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include pale pink, peach, creamy white, light blue, lavender purple, mint green, warm yellow, soft gray, and light brown. High-saturation pastels may also be used, but they should remain matte.

Shadows can use blue-violet, gray-pink, or warm brown. Dead black is not ideal. The overall color should be gentle, refined, and misted.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 9. Chinese Ink｜Eastern Ink Wash and Negative Space Style

### Style Core

The core of Chinese ink painting is ink tonality, brush energy, negative space, qi rhythm, paper absorption, and expressing more with less. It does not rely on complete realistic detail. Instead, it uses ink lines, texture strokes, rubbing, washes, dry brush, wet brush, and tonal variation to express both form and spirit.

For image generation, the focus is the relationship between ink and water on rice paper. The subject can be landscape, figure, flower-and-bird, animal, architecture, or abstract imagery, but it must retain ink tonality, brush method, and negative space. It should not become a black-and-white photograph.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should show rice paper texture, ink bleeding, and water diffusion. Ink lines may vary between dry and wet. Dense ink should feel black and powerful; pale ink should be transparent, gentle, and water-rich. Brushstrokes should show the beginning, movement, and ending of the brush, along with dry-brush streaks, broken-brush effects, texture strokes, dots, and washes.

Composition should value negative space. Blank areas are not empty; they represent mist, water, sky, distance, or breath. Rocks and mountains may be described with texture strokes. Branches may use dry brush. Petals may use pale ink or minimal color. Figures may be simplified into concise lines. The image does not need to be filled; subject and emptiness should breathe together.

Chinese ink painting values mood and spirit. It should not be overly three-dimensional, overly realistic, or overly decorative. Edges can naturally bleed, and distant elements can fade into pale ink. Overall, the image should be elegant, restrained, fluid, and full of brush spirit, as if ink has truly fallen onto rice paper.

### Color Vocabulary

Main tones include dense ink, pale ink, gray ink, rice paper white, ocher, pale blue-green, pale green, and cinnabar seal red. Color is highly restrained, with ink variation as the main language.

A small amount of ink-and-color wash may be used, but high saturation is not ideal. A red seal can act as a rhythmic accent.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

## 10. Chinese Style｜Classical Eastern Decorative and Poetic Style

### Style Core

The core of Chinese style is Eastern poetic sensibility, traditional cultural symbols, elegant composition, negative space, landscape mood, classical objects, and decorative order. It does not necessarily mean pure ink painting. It may combine meticulous painting, blue-green landscape, palace decoration, classical architecture, flower-and-bird motifs, cloud patterns, silk, screens, lattice windows, calligraphy, and seals.

For image generation, the focus is Chinese classical aesthetics and Eastern mood. Any subject can be transformed into a restrained, elegant, poetic image full of traditional cultural symbols.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should have a Chinese classical compositional sensibility, valuing emptiness and fullness, density and openness, negative space, and layered depth. It may include distant mountains, mist, pine, bamboo, plum blossoms, lotus, peonies, pavilions, terraces, screens, scrolls, lattice windows, porcelain, jade, silk, cloud patterns, wave patterns, calligraphic inscriptions, and cinnabar seals. Colors may be pale and refined, or they may use blue-green, cinnabar red, and gold to create a more ornate classical atmosphere. The background should not look like a modern tourism poster. It should carry the texture of a scroll, paper, silk, or mural. Overall, the image should be elegant, inward, cultured, poetic, and rich in Eastern decorative beauty.

## 11. Ukiyo-e｜Japanese Edo Woodblock Flat Color Style

### Style Core

The core of ukiyo-e is the flat composition, clear outlines, registered color printing, decorative rhythm, and everyday-life, landscape, and figure subjects of Edo-period Japanese woodblock prints. It does not rely on Western realistic light and shadow. Instead, it builds the image through line, flat color, pattern, and composition.

For image generation, the focus is woodblock printing texture, flat color areas, clear outlines, and Japanese print composition. Any subject can be transformed into an Edo print language.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should have clear dark contour lines. Each shape should be outlined and then filled with flat color. It should not use heavy oil-paint shadows or photographic depth of field. Colors may show the slight unevenness of woodblock registration, edge misalignment, and pigment absorption into paper.

Composition may use bold cropping, strong foreground elements, diagonal movement, simplified distant views, decorative waves, bridges, mountains, travelers, geisha, samurai, streets, flowers and birds, rain, snow, clouds, and mist. The image can be highly rhythmic, with clear contrast between foreground and background.

Faces and clothing folds should be simplified and linear. Clothing can contain flat patterns. Natural elements such as waves, water, clouds, and branches can become decorative, forming rhythmic line patterns. Overall, the image should resemble a real woodblock print, with paper fibers, pigment grain, registration traces, and flat graphic beauty.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include indigo, Prussian blue, pale gray, ivory white, ocher, ink black, light red, tea brown, and soft yellow. Colors are crisp, flat, and medium in saturation.

There should be very little shading. The image should rely mainly on line and color-block relationships. Avoid heavy black shadows and modern neon color.

## 12. Woodcut｜Black-and-white Carved Print Style

### Style Core

The core of woodcut and printmaking is carved marks, black-and-white contrast, printed grain, rough lines, and reproducible image quality. It can refer to woodcut, engraving, linocut, or related printing effects, but here the focus leans toward the strong visual language of woodcut relief printing.

For image generation, the key is “carved lines” rather than merely drawn lines. The image should have knife marks, reserved white areas, pressure marks, uneven ink, and a paper-printed surface.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should have strong black-and-white or limited-color contrast. Forms should be made from carved lines of varying thickness, black masses, white cuts, and scratches. Lines should have the hard, irreversible quality of a cutting tool. They should not feel soft like pencil or perfectly smooth like vector lines. Black areas may feel heavy, while white lines should look as if carved out of black ink.

Shadows can be built with parallel carved lines, cross-carving, short knife marks, rough texture, and black-and-white blocks. Figures, animals, landscapes, architecture, and symbolic images can all be simplified into high-contrast graphics. The composition should have graphic force, suitable for social themes, folk subjects, allegory, posters, expressionist imagery, or strong narrative scenes.

Print texture is essential: edges can be slightly uneven, ink may contain missed spots, pressure marks, paper grain, and roughness. Overall, the image should have the force, simplicity, and black-and-white impact of hand-carved printmaking.

### Color Vocabulary

## 13. Paper-cut｜Flat Folk Cut-paper Silhouette Style

### Style Core

The core of paper-cut art is cut paper, hollowed patterns, symmetry, flatness, folk ornament, and symbolic shapes. It is not based on painted light and shadow. Instead, it looks as if shapes have been cut from paper with scissors or a knife, using positive and negative space, edge contours, and hollow motifs to build the image.

For image generation, the focus is paper layering, cut edges, hollowed patterns, and folk decoration. The subject should be simplified into cuttable shapes and should not depend on overly realistic detail.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should clearly show paper material, with edges that may contain small fibers, scissor marks, or slightly imperfect knife-cut irregularities. Patterns should be built from flat silhouettes, hollow ornaments, symmetrical structures, repeating motifs, radial designs, or layered paper pieces. Figures, animals, plants, architecture, and objects should all be simplified into silhouette-like shapes.

Positive and negative space are extremely important. Blank areas should look like holes cut out of the paper, forming patterns together with the paper surface itself. Common forms include red paper-cut designs, window flowers, folk auspicious motifs, flowers, birds, fish, insects, zodiac animals, cloud patterns, wave patterns, figure profiles, and festival decorations. Compositions may be symmetrical, circular, square, horizontal, or full-page.

The image should not contain photographic lighting or complex three-dimensional shading. Slight paper-layer shadows may be used to show stacked paper, but the overall effect should remain flat and decorative. The image should feel handmade, folk-based, clear, graphic, and warm, with the crispness and humanity of cut paper.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include cinnabar red, paper white, black, gold, pink, blue-green, and earth yellow. Traditional paper-cutting is most strongly associated with red, though layered colored paper can also be used.

Colors should be flat, clean, and saturated. Large gradients are not ideal. The paper itself may have fibers and a matte surface.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

## 14. Stained Glass｜Sacred Window and Backlit Glass Style

### Style Core

The core of stained glass is glass segmentation, lead outlines, transmitted light, sacred window design, and image construction through colored glass pieces. It is not ordinary colorful illustration. It should feel as if light is passing through colored glass, giving the image a jewel-like transparency.

For image generation, the focus is lead framing, glass texture, backlighting, color-block assembly, and window structure. Any subject should be transformed into an image assembled from glass pieces.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should have clear black or dark lead lines dividing the subject and background into many pieces of glass. Each glass section can have varying transparency, color density, bubbles, cracks, flow lines, or handmade glass unevenness. Colors should look as though light is shining through from behind, creating an internal glow.

Composition may resemble a church window, circular rose window, arched window, geometric window grid, saint-like icon composition, or decorative window ornament. Figures, animals, flowers, landscapes, and abstract forms should be simplified into glass color blocks. Shadows are not oil-paint shadows; they are created through differences in glass color and light transmission.

Light beams, window frames, wall projections, or colorful light spots cast on the floor may appear, but the stained-glass image should remain the visual center. Overall, it should feel ornate, sacred, transparent, and jewel-like, with handmade glass and leaded assembly.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include jewel blue, deep red, golden yellow, emerald green, purple, cyan-blue, orange, white, and black lead outlines. Colors should be saturated and luminous, as if light is passing through glass.

Avoid gray dullness and plastic neon. Highlights should feel like glass reflection and transmission, not metallic shine.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 15. Mosaic｜Fragmented Tile and Tesserae Inlay Style

### Style Core

The core of mosaic is fragmented assembly, inlay, hard material particles, grid-like structure, and a complete image formed from many small pieces. It can resemble ancient Roman walls, Byzantine golden icons, modern tile murals, or stone-and-glass compositions.

For image generation, the focus is that the image is composed of many small pieces of material. The subject should not merely have a grid filter placed over it; it should genuinely appear assembled from stones, glass, ceramic pieces, or tiles.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should consist of many small fragments. Each piece may have a slightly different shape, such as square, trapezoid, irregular stone, glass, or ceramic tile. Clear gaps, grout lines, or inlay boundaries should appear between pieces. From a distance, the image forms a complete picture; up close, the material particles should be visible.

The arrangement of fragments should follow the direction of the form. In a face, fragments should follow facial planes; in fabric folds, they should form flowing lines; in the background, they can radiate, ripple, or form geometric structures. Avoid merely creating a uniform pixelated filter. Mosaic requires handmade assembly order and material variation.

Materials may show rough stone texture, glass reflection, matte ceramic surface, small gold-leaf pieces, or cracks in an old wall. Color can be rich, but it must be built from small pieces. Overall, the image should feel hard, ancient, decorative, assembled, and wall-like.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include gold, jewel blue, ocher red, earth yellow, ivory white, ink black, emerald green, gray, and terracotta. A Byzantine direction can be more golden and luxurious, while an ancient Roman direction can lean toward earth tones and stone.

Colors should appear as individual material pieces, not smooth gradients.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 16. Embroidery｜Thread, Fiber, and Fabric Stitch Style

### Style Core

The core of embroidery is thread, stitches, fabric, fibers, hand sewing, and a patterned surface. It is not made with paint. It is an image sewn into cloth with needle and thread, built through thread direction, stitch density, fiber sheen, and textile texture.

For image generation, the focus is that the image is genuinely stitched onto fabric. The subject should be made from stitches and thread segments, not merely a normal illustration with a fabric texture filter.

### Visual Characteristics

The image should clearly show fabric as the base material, such as cotton, linen, canvas, or silk. The surface should have warp and weft texture, fiber fuzz, and slight unevenness. The image itself should be made from embroidery thread, with each thread direction, thickness, and slight sheen visible. Different areas may use running stitch, satin stitch, chain stitch, long-and-short stitch, cross-stitch, French knots, or related effects.

Stitch direction should follow the form. Petals may use radial stitches, hair may use long and short threads, fabric folds may follow the cloth’s flow, and backgrounds may be filled with regular stitches or leave the fabric exposed. Edges should not look as perfect as digital vector graphics. They may show thread ends, fuzz, slight misalignment, and handmade irregularity.

Embroidery can be refined or folk-like. The composition may resemble a patch, fabric design, handkerchief, wall hanging, clothing embroidery, flowers and birds, figures, text, or decorative symbols. Overall, it should have a soft, fibrous, tactile quality, as if the threads are raised from the cloth surface.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include cotton white, ivory fabric base, red thread, blue thread, green thread, gold thread, pink, earth yellow, black thread, and dark brown. Colors may be vivid or softly vintage.

Thread can have a slight silk sheen, but it should not become plastically glossy. The base fabric color strongly affects the atmosphere and may be beige linen, deep blue cloth, black cloth, or white cloth.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Embroidery handcraft style, image built from stitched threads one needle at a time, fabric warp and weft texture and fibers clearly visible, stitch arrangements showing direction and thickness, visible running stitch, satin stitch, long-and-short stitch, chain stitch, and cross-stitch effects, thread slightly raised from the surface with subtle fiber sheen, handmade thread ends and slight irregularities along the edges, ivory fabric base, red thread, blue thread, green thread, gold thread, pink, black thread, and dark brown palette, overall warm, soft, handmade, and tactile with real fabric embroidery texture, not using ordinary digital illustration or printed pattern, but building the image through stitches, thread, and textile fibers.

### Negative Prompt
