## 1. Anime｜Japanese Anime Style

### Style Core

The core of anime style is highly designed character construction, clean contour lines, expressive but readable emotions, and a visual language that sits between flat illustration and animated storytelling. It does not aim for complete photographic realism. Instead, it turns characters, scenes, actions, and emotions into clearer, more symbolic, more dramatic images.

For image generation, the focus should be character recognizability and emotional expression. Facial features, hairstyle, gaze, pose, costume silhouette, and body language should all be clear. The image should feel like a key visual, character illustration, or dramatic moment from an animated work.

### Visual Features

Characters usually have large eyes, distinct pupil highlights, refined hair clusters, clean linework, and simplified but appealing facial structure. Expressions can be stronger and more readable than realistic portraits: determined, shy, angry, surprised, distant, gentle, energetic, or heroic. The expression should be clear without facial collapse or uncontrolled distortion.

Linework is the structural backbone of the style. Outer contours should be smooth and clean, while hair strands, clothing folds, gestures, and accessories can be defined with thinner supporting lines. Shading often uses cel shading or soft animation-style shadow shapes. Dark areas should be clear and simplified rather than physically perfect. Backgrounds may include cities, schools, fantasy settings, interiors, or natural landscapes, but they should support the character’s emotion instead of overpowering the subject.

Composition may use strong character-centered framing, dynamic poses, tilted camera angles, close-ups, half-body portraits, full-body character sheets, or poster-like layouts. The image should feel clean, readable, narrative, and emotionally charged. It should not drift into generic 3D game rendering or Western cartoon styling.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include clean high-saturation hues, soft skin tones, bright hair colors, school-uniform colors, sky blue, cherry-blossom pink, deep blue shadows, warm yellow lighting, purple night tones, and crisp white highlights. The color can be vivid, but it should retain a layered animation look.

Shadows often lean blue-violet, gray-blue, or warm brown, and should not become muddy. The palette may shift toward energetic, fresh, sci-fi, school-life, fantasy, or dark themes, but it should still preserve clean anime linework and strong character readability.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, 3D render, plastic CGI skin, Western cartoon face, overly realistic pores, low-quality hands, broken facial features, asymmetrical eyes, rough linework, muddy colors, heavy oil painting texture, realistic photographic depth of field, horror deformation, unreadable character design.

## 2. Animation｜Animated Film Style

### Style Core

The core of animation style is clear, fluid, motion-ready design. It is not limited to Japanese anime; it may include hand-drawn animation, digital animation, animated film background art, concept art, and character animation language. The emphasis is on simplified shapes, clear emotions, living movement, and images that can support storytelling.

For image generation, the subject should feel like a frame from an animated film. Character, setting, lighting, and composition should all have narrative function. The image does not need to be extremely realistic, but it must be clean, readable, warm, and full of movement potential.

### Visual Features

Characters are usually designed and simplified, with clear silhouettes and readable poses. Human figures, animals, and creatures may use exaggerated proportions, but the exaggeration should feel stable, intentional, and performative. Gestures, gaze direction, body orientation, and center of gravity must be clear, so the viewer can sense what the character is thinking, doing, or about to do.

Backgrounds often have the feeling of animation production design: simplified perspective, clear foreground-midground-background separation, soft lighting, readable color blocks, and clean spatial organization. Materials may be more idealized than reality. Wood, stone, cloth, clouds, water, and foliage can be expressed through simplified brushwork and broad color masses.

The composition should feel like a storyboard frame or cinematic animation shot, with deliberate eye direction and narrative staging. Lighting can be dramatic, but it should not become visually chaotic like unplanned real photography. The whole image needs story, rhythm, action, and emotion rather than static product-display stillness.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include clear warm tones, soft ambient light, sky blue, grass green, warm orange, cream white, soft pink, deep purple shadows, and golden highlights. The color is usually cleaner and more idealized than reality, with an obvious dominant palette.

The palette can be warm, adventurous, magical, humorous, or melancholy depending on the story, but the color organization should remain clear. Avoid dirty, purposeless saturation and visual clutter.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, overly complex texture, plastic 3D look, stiff pose, empty expression, cluttered scene, low readability, excessive darkness, no story feeling, photographic noise, heavy oil paint, broken character proportions, background overpowering the subject.

## 3. Comic｜Comic Panel and Print Style

### Style Core

The core of comic style is visual storytelling, expressive linework, panel rhythm, and print language. It is not simply a character drawing; the image should feel like a comic page, cover, single-panel story, or graphic novel insert. The emphasis is on strong line art, bold silhouettes, dynamic composition, emotional symbols, and sequential-art energy.

For image generation, any subject can be transformed into a comic image. The main subject should have clear action, the scene should contain dramatic tension, and light and line should serve the story rather than photographic accuracy.

### Visual Features

Line is the most important expressive tool. Contour lines may vary in thickness, and dark areas may be treated with hatching, cross-hatching, solid black shapes, or halftone dots. Action scenes may include speed lines, impact lines, sound-effect lettering, exaggerated perspective, or intense framing. Static scenes can use panel-like cropping, black borders, speech balloons, caption boxes, or cover-style composition.

Facial expressions and poses can be more exaggerated than realism. Brows, eyes, mouth shapes, hand gestures, and body direction should be readable. The level of detail can shift depending on the comic subgenre: American comics may use heavier black lines and muscular structure; Japanese manga may emphasize finer lines and screentone; European comics may emphasize location detail and color-block design.

The surface should have print qualities such as halftone texture, paper grain, ink edges, and flat color blocks. It should not become glossy 3D or ordinary smooth illustration.

### Color Vocabulary

The palette may use black-and-white contrast, four-color print effects, halftone reds, blues, and yellows, strong shadow black, bright red, deep blue, vivid yellow, ink black, and paper white. Color comics should rely on flat color areas and clear shadow shapes.

Black-and-white comics should emphasize ink lines, blank space, screentone, and solid black masses. The overall color or tonal structure should feel printed and graphically forceful.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Comic style, strong clear ink outlines, varied line weight, panel-like dramatic composition, image with narrative moment and dynamic tension, hatching, cross-hatching, halftone dots, speed lines, impact lines and printed paper texture, clear expressive character faces and poses, flat color blocks with high-contrast shadows, image resembling a comic cover, single comic panel, or graphic novel illustration, strong storytelling and print-based visual energy.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, 3D render, glossy CGI, no linework, no halftone, no story feeling, soft-focus oil painting, low contrast, overly realistic skin, commercial photography, no panel-like composition, messy linework, random text artifacts, unreadable expressions.

## 4. Cartoon｜Exaggerated Cartoon Style

### Style Core

The core of cartoon style is simplification, exaggeration, humor, shape design, and high readability. It is more symbolic than anime and much freer than realism. It often uses rounded forms, exaggerated proportions, direct expressions, and a light rhythm. The goal is not realism, but immediate visual understanding.

For image generation, any subject can become a friendly, humorous, exaggerated, elastic cartoon image. Shapes should be bolder, emotions more direct, and actions more flexible.

### Visual Features

Characters, animals, and objects may have big eyes, large heads, small bodies, rounded hands and feet, simplified facial features, and exaggerated expressions. Lines are usually bold, clean, and clearly readable. Forms may stretch, squash, bend, or curve with the elastic feeling of animated squash-and-stretch motion.

Backgrounds should be simple and clear, without complex realistic texture. Objects can be geometric, rounded, or symbolic. Expression is central: happiness, surprise, mischief, confusion, fear, or excitement should be immediately understandable. Shading is usually simple, with flat color blocks.

The composition works well for posters, stickers, children’s media, short animation, or humorous illustration. The image should not become too dark, too realistic, or overly adult in tone.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include bright primaries, soft candy colors, bright yellow, sky blue, grass green, orange, pink, pure white, and black outlines. Color should be direct, cheerful, and clear, without complex gray gradients.

Shadows can be simple darker versions of the base colors, preserving a flat clean look. The overall feeling should be lively, readable, and friendly.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, realistic horror, plastic 3D toy look, overly complex textures, heavy oil painting, dark violent mood, unclear expression, too much detail, realistic photographic lighting, low-saturation dull colors, stiff proportions, no humor.

## 5. Ghibli｜Hand-drawn Japanese Animated Film Art Style

### Style Core

This style centers on warm hand-drawn animation, poetic coexistence of nature and daily life, soft lighting, detailed background art, and characters and environments that seem to breathe. It is not merely cute animation; it is a film-art language that blends everyday life, nature, growth, adventure, and gentle nostalgia.

For image generation, the focus should be hand-drawn background art and a warm world-view. The image should feel like a frame full of air, wind, grass, water vapor, and lived-in detail rather than glossy 3D or a generic anime portrait.

### Visual Features

The background is extremely important. Forests, meadows, clouds, rural houses, old streets, seaside stations, flying machines, kitchens, greenhouses, and mysterious natural spaces should all have detailed hand-painted feeling. Plants should be rich but not mechanical, clouds and skies should be soft, and buildings should show traces of everyday life.

Character design is usually simple, natural, and emotionally sincere. Characters should not be overly sexualized or dressed in excessive decorative costumes. Proportions can be animation-realistic; facial features are simple but alive. Wind is often present visually: moving hair, fluttering clothing edges, bending grass, drifting clouds.

Lighting should be soft and natural, such as morning light, afternoon light, dusk, or overcast diffuse light. The scene may contain many details, but it should never feel cluttered. The overall mood should suggest childhood memory, natural mystery, domestic warmth, and the quiet before an adventure.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include grass green, sky blue, cloud white, warm beige, soft orange, warm brown, gentle gray, forest green, lake blue, earth tones, and natural skin colors. The palette should be fresh but not fluorescent, like hand-painted animation background pigment.

The overall color can lean natural, warm, and nostalgic. Shadows should be soft rather than cold black. The light should resemble natural daylight in hand-drawn animated films.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

## 6. Pixel Art｜Pixel Art Style

### Style Core

The core of pixel art is building an image through clearly visible pixel blocks rather than continuous blurred brushwork. The image is composed of small square color units, using limited resolution, limited palettes, and precise placement to create characters, environments, lighting, and texture. It belongs to the visual language of retro games, 8-bit, 16-bit, handheld games, and indie game art.

For image generation, the pixel grid must not disappear. The image should not be a normal illustration with a mosaic filter; the shape design, edges, shadows, and textures must all obey pixel logic.

### Visual Features

Edges should show stair-stepped pixelated contours rather than excessive anti-aliasing. Characters and objects should be simplified into clear silhouettes, with details suggested by a small number of placed pixels. Eyes, buttons, glints, highlights, and shadows should all be built from distinct color blocks.

Possible compositions include isometric views, side-scrolling scenes, top-down RPG views, platform-game layouts, retro interface screens, game environments, and character sprites. Backgrounds may include tile-based floors, repeated patterns, square clouds, pixel trees, pixel cities, and low-resolution lighting effects.

Light and shadow should be handled with limited steps, often three to five value levels. Materials should never become finely airbrushed. The overall result must be clean, sharp, grid-based, retro, and readable.

### Color Vocabulary

Use limited palettes: 8-bit primaries, 16-bit saturated colors, retro handheld green, neon blue-violet, warm orange, deep indigo, black outlines, and bright white highlights. Color quantity should be controlled, with minimal gradients.

Shadows should be layered by hard color blocks, not soft blur. The palette can shift toward retro game, cute pixel, dark pixel, or cyber pixel depending on the subject.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Pixel art style, clearly visible low-resolution pixel grid, stair-stepped pixelated edges, limited color palette, clear silhouettes, characters and objects built from square pixel color blocks, shadows rendered as layered pixel blocks without smooth gradients, image resembling an 8-bit or 16-bit retro game scene, character sprite, or indie game art, readable composition, sharp visible pixels, nostalgic game feeling, handcrafted pixel-placement texture.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, smooth digital illustration, excessive anti-aliasing, soft focus, 3D render, CGI, oil painting brushwork, no visible pixels, overly high-resolution detail, blurry mosaic filter, unlimited palette, real photographic lighting, plastic texture.

## 7. Photoreal｜Photorealistic Style

### Style Core

The core of photoreal style is making the image look like real photography or high-end realistic rendering. It emphasizes lighting, materials, proportions, anatomy, perspective, lens behavior, environmental detail, and natural imperfections. It should not look like illustration, animation, or symbolic graphic design. The viewer should believe that the subject exists in a physically photographable space.

For image generation, the focus is credibility. The subject must have correct material response, natural light and shadow, real scale, and believable interaction with the environment.

### Visual Features

Human figures need realistic skin texture, including pores, fine lines, subtle imperfections, natural color variation, and plausible eye highlights. Clothing should show real fabric folds and material behavior. Metal, glass, wood, leather, stone, water, and other materials must respond physically to light.

The lighting must have a clear source: natural window light, overcast diffuse light, dusk backlight, studio softbox, or night artificial light. Shadow direction, reflections, highlights, and contact shadows should be consistent. Backgrounds should not look pasted on; foreground and background depth of field, focal length, perspective, and atmospheric distance should agree.

The composition can resemble DSLR photography, film stills, phone photography, studio portraiture, documentary photography, or product photography. The image should retain natural imperfections such as slight noise, lens depth, believable edges, and non-plastic detail.

### Color Vocabulary

Colors should respond to the light source and environment: natural skin tones, environmental reflected colors, real white balance, blue-gray shadows, warm orange light, city night colors, natural greens, and sky blues. The color should not be overly flat or cartoon-like.

Color grading may be cinematic, documentary, soft commercial, or natural daylight, but it should remain physically plausible.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

Avoid anime, cartoon, illustration, oil painting, watercolor, flat vector art, plastic CGI, uncanny skin, broken anatomy, impossible lighting, inconsistent shadows, fake background, over-smoothed skin, excessive beauty filter, painterly brushstrokes, unrealistic materials, low-resolution detail.

## 8. Impressionist｜Impressionist Light and Color Style

### Style Core

The core of impressionist style is the perception of light at a specific moment rather than the fixed outline of objects. The image should not describe every detail precisely; instead, it should use loose brushstrokes, broken color, and optical vibration to capture outdoor air, changing light, reflections, and atmosphere.

For image generation, the focus should be “momentary light.” Any subject can be transformed into a scene shaped by sun, mist, reflection, shade, and color vibration.

### Visual Features

Contours should be soft and open. Trees, water, buildings, figures, flowers, and sky should be dissolved into short strokes and broken touches of color. Edges may be loose; objects should feel as if they are trembling inside the surrounding air.

The brushwork should remain visible. Short strokes, dabs, and adjacent color patches build the image. Water reflections, gardens, riversides, hayfields, bridges, morning fog, afternoon light, and open-air scenes are especially suitable. Shadows should not be painted with pure black; they should be built from blue, violet, green-gray, pale pink, and other complementary colors.

The composition can be quiet, but the surface must feel alive. The image should feel fresh, bright, fluid, and atmospheric, as if nature has been changed by light in one brief instant.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include pale blue, lavender, grass green, soft yellow, cream white, rose pink, light gray, sky blue, watery green, and warm sunlight. The palette should be bright but airy, with broken color rather than solid flat fills.

Shadows should be colorful and transparent, usually cool blue-violet or green-gray. Avoid heavy black and muddy brown.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Impressionist painting style, loose visible brushstrokes, broken color, soft dissolving edges, outdoor light and air, shimmering reflections, color vibration, momentary natural atmosphere, subjects formed by short strokes and dabs rather than hard outlines, blue-violet and green-gray shadows instead of black, fresh bright palette, airy plein-air feeling, luminous daylight, painterly texture, image capturing the sensation of light changing at a specific moment.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid photorealism, hard digital edges, heavy black shadows, 3D render, CGI, anime, cartoon, overly detailed linework, smooth airbrush, flat vector art, plastic texture, harsh studio lighting, rigid outlines, muddy colors, no visible brushwork.

## 9. Post-Impressionist｜Post-Impressionist Subjective Color Style

### Style Core

Post-impressionist style extends beyond pure optical light and uses subjective color, visible structure, expressive brushwork, and symbolic mood. The image may still contain landscapes, portraits, interiors, or still lifes, but these are transformed by personal emotion, decorative simplification, stronger outline, and more deliberate composition.

For image generation, the focus is not natural accuracy but expressive color and painterly construction. The subject should feel shaped by inner feeling, structure, and rhythm.

### Visual Features

Brushwork should be more deliberate than impressionism. Strokes may be thick, directional, patterned, rhythmic, or structural. Forms can be simplified and outlined, color areas can be flattened, and perspective can become more subjective. The surface should not feel like a soft natural snapshot; it should feel composed and emotionally charged.

Depending on direction, the image may emphasize bold impasto strokes, symbolic flat color, simplified planes, decorative outlines, or geometric structure. Trees, roads, houses, faces, skies, and still-life objects can become more intense, more ordered, or more psychologically expressive than real life.

The composition should have stronger design than impressionism. Color contrast, line direction, and shapes should guide emotion. The image should feel like painted reality transformed by an artist’s internal logic.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include cobalt blue, deep ultramarine, yellow ochre, chrome yellow, orange, emerald green, vermilion, purple, wine red, earth brown, and strong black outlines depending on the substyle. Color can be high-contrast and subjective.

Avoid purely natural local color. Shadows may be blue, purple, green, or red depending on emotional design.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

### Negative Prompt

## 10. Expressionist｜Emotional Distortion Style

### Style Core

Expressionist style externalizes emotion through distortion, intense color, rough brushwork, and unstable composition. The image should not calmly reproduce the world; it should make anxiety, anger, fear, loneliness, spiritual intensity, or psychological pressure visible through form and color.

For image generation, the subject should feel pushed out of ordinary reality by emotion. Faces, bodies, landscapes, rooms, and skies can all bend under inner pressure.

### Visual Features

Forms may be elongated, compressed, twisted, or exaggerated. Faces can become mask-like, eyes hollow, hands tense, bodies angular or stretched. Backgrounds should not be neutral decoration; they can swirl, tilt, close in, or vibrate around the subject.

Brushwork should be visible, rough, urgent, and emotionally loaded. Lines may be jagged, dark, nervous, or heavy. Composition can use diagonal pressure, claustrophobic framing, isolated figures, compressed interiors, or distorted landscapes. The goal is not ugliness for its own sake, but emotional truth made visual.

The image should avoid polished prettiness. It should carry tension, spiritual unrest, alienation, or psychological force. Even still scenes should feel charged.

### Color Vocabulary

Colors are often intense, unnatural, and emotionally symbolic: blood red, acid yellow, deep blue, black, sickly green, orange, violet, gray-purple, and harsh white. Shadows may be colored rather than realistic.

Color should serve emotion. It may be aggressive, anxious, feverish, mournful, or spiritually intense.

### Negative Prompt

## 11. Surreal｜Surreal Dream Style

### Style Core

Surreal style combines impossible imagery with dream logic. Objects, bodies, landscapes, and spaces may be precisely rendered yet conceptually irrational. The image should feel like a calm but disturbing dream, where ordinary things appear in impossible relationships.

For image generation, the focus is not random weirdness, but symbolic contradiction. The image should be strange, memorable, psychologically suggestive, and visually coherent even when impossible.

### Visual Features

Scenes may include floating objects, melting forms, impossible architecture, oversized everyday items, doors opening into skies, bodies transforming into objects, distorted scale, empty deserts, mirrors, clocks, eyes, eggs, insects, masks, or double images. Elements should have crisp edges and convincing material detail, making the impossible feel physically present.

Space can be vast, silent, and sharply lit, or soft and dreamlike. Long shadows, distant horizons, empty rooms, uncanny interiors, and mismatched objects are useful. The composition should feel deliberate, not like random fantasy clutter.

The mood should be quiet, uncanny, symbolic, and slightly unsettling. Avoid turning it into generic horror or fantasy action.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include desert beige, pale blue sky, cold gray, deep black, muted flesh tones, warm ochre, dusty brown, ivory, surreal red, and clear shadow blue. Some surreal images may use soft dream colors, but the palette should remain controlled.

Color should enhance the dream logic: clean, strange, silent, and symbolic rather than chaotic neon.

### Negative Prompt

## 12. Cubist｜Cubist Multi-view Geometric Style

### Style Core

Cubist style breaks objects and space into planes, facets, and multiple viewpoints. Instead of presenting a subject from one correct perspective, it shows front, side, top, profile, and structural fragments at the same time. The image becomes an analytical reconstruction of form.

For image generation, the subject should be decomposed and rebuilt into geometric planes. It should not become random abstraction; it should preserve structural logic and multi-view organization.

### Visual Features

Faces may contain front-facing eyes, profile noses, displaced mouths, and asymmetrical features. Bodies and objects may be split into angular planes. Background and subject can interpenetrate, with space flattened into a mosaic of overlapping facets.

Still-life subjects may include bottles, instruments, newspapers, tables, chairs, windows, and fragmented shapes. Portraits should show distortion, analysis, and structural reassembly. Edges can be sharp, angular, and planar; shadows are often built from tonal facets rather than realistic light.

The composition should feel intellectual, experimental, fragmented, and modernist. Avoid ordinary abstract scribbles. Cubism needs clear geometric logic, compressed space, and multiple viewpoints.

### Color Vocabulary

Analytical cubism often uses ochre, gray, black, beige, brown, muted blue, and off-white. Later or synthetic cubist versions may include stronger red, yellow, green, and patterned collage effects.

Color should support planes and structure rather than natural lighting.

### Negative Prompt

## 13. Art Nouveau｜Art Nouveau Curvilinear Decorative Style

### Style Core

Art Nouveau is built on flowing curves, organic ornament, elegant figures, decorative frames, and a fusion of nature and design. It often feels like a refined poster, book cover, stained-glass panel, or ornamental illustration. The image should prioritize graceful line, botanical rhythm, and integrated decorative structure.

For image generation, any subject can be transformed into a refined decorative composition surrounded by curves, flowers, vines, halos, arches, and flowing hair or fabric.

### Visual Features

Lines should be clean, elegant, and sinuous. Hair, fabric, plant stems, smoke, water, and ornaments can all become long flowing curves. Figures often appear in central or symmetrical composition with graceful gestures and elongated silhouettes. Surrounding elements may include floral borders, vines, circular halos, arched frames, stylized typography panels, and decorative pattern bands.

The image should be relatively flat and ornamental rather than deeply realistic. Shading should be soft and limited. The figure and decoration should feel unified; the background is not separate but part of the graphic design.

Composition should be carefully framed. The top, bottom, and sides can contain botanical patterning, curved geometry, or poster-like borders, but the design must remain elegant rather than cluttered.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include cream, pale gold, sage green, dusty pink, lavender, muted teal, soft brown, ivory, and fine dark outlines. Colors should be flat, gentle, and refined, with a vintage print feeling.

The palette should feel romantic, elegant, and decorative rather than neon or overly photographic.

## 14. Art Deco｜Geometric Luxury Style

### Style Core

Art Deco centers on geometric order, luxury, symmetry, metallic decoration, architectural rhythm, and modern glamour. It is cleaner and more angular than Art Nouveau. The image should feel elegant, urban, expensive, structured, and stylish.

For image generation, the subject should be placed into a world of stepped geometry, sunbursts, fan shapes, streamlined forms, polished materials, and decorative symmetry.

### Visual Features

Shapes should be sharp and orderly: zigzags, chevrons, stepped forms, rays, fans, arches, symmetrical frames, vertical towers, and streamlined curves. Human figures can appear elongated, elegant, poised, and fashion-like, but not overly casual. Buildings and interiors may resemble grand theaters, skyscrapers, luxury hotels, or vintage posters.

Surfaces should suggest polished metal, lacquer, marble, glass, satin, and mirrored decoration. Composition often uses central symmetry, strong verticals, and precise geometric framing. Details should be decorative but controlled.

The image should not feel handmade and organic like Art Nouveau; it should feel modern, architectural, glamorous, and mechanically refined.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include gold, black, ivory, emerald green, deep navy, burgundy, champagne, bronze, silver, and cream. High contrast between black and metallic gold is especially characteristic.

The palette should feel luxurious, polished, and theatrical, not soft rural or childish.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

## 31. Kawaii｜Japanese Cute Style

### Style Core

Kawaii style is built on cuteness, softness, friendliness, round shapes, gentle emotion, and approachable design. It is more than simply drawing something small; the whole image should reduce threat, simplify form, and increase charm, innocence, and warmth.

For image generation, any subject can be softened into a cute, rounded, friendly version with simplified details and sweet emotional readability.

### Visual Features

Characters often have rounded heads, small bodies, large eyes, tiny mouths, soft cheeks, simple limbs, and compact silhouettes. Animals, objects, food, plants, or even machines can receive cute facial features and rounded proportions. Expressions should be gentle, shy, happy, curious, sleepy, or sweet.

Lines should be smooth and simple. Forms should avoid sharp aggression. Backgrounds may include hearts, stars, sparkles, clouds, bows, plush textures, pastel patterns, stickers, or soft decorative motifs. The image should be clean, friendly, and emotionally light.

The style should not become overly complex or realistic. It should feel like stickers, mascots, cute stationery, plush toys, or cheerful character goods.

### Color Vocabulary

Common colors include pastel pink, baby blue, lavender, cream, mint green, soft yellow, peach, white, and gentle brown. High saturation can be used, but usually in a soft candy-like way.

Shadows should be minimal and gentle. Avoid harsh black or dirty gray.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Kawaii style, cute rounded shapes, soft friendly character design, large gentle eyes, tiny mouth, small body proportions, smooth simple lines, pastel colors, soft cheeks, sweet readable expression, hearts, stars, sparkles, bows or soft decorative motifs, clean cheerful composition, pastel pink, baby blue, lavender, mint, cream, soft yellow and peach palette, innocent, charming, friendly, sticker-like or mascot-like visual language.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid horror, aggressive sharp shapes, photorealistic skin, gritty realism, dark gore, harsh shadows, dirty colors, complex adult detail, intimidating expression, 3D plastic uncanny look, violent weapons, gloomy mood, no cuteness, no rounded form.

## 32. Monochrome｜Black-and-white Monochrome Style

### Style Core

Monochrome style limits the image to black, white, and grayscale, or to a single-color tonal range. Its strength comes from value, contrast, texture, composition, and light rather than hue. The style can be photographic, ink-based, graphic, cinematic, or painterly depending on execution.

For image generation, the subject should be designed through light and dark structure. Color should not carry the image; tonal relationships must do the work.

### Visual Features

The image should have clear value organization. Highlights, midtones, shadows, and blacks should be deliberate. Strong silhouettes, dramatic lighting, ink-like linework, grayscale gradients, film grain, paper texture, or charcoal marks can all support the style.

Depending on direction, monochrome may look like black-and-white photography, ink drawing, noir cinema, pencil sketch, charcoal study, engraving, or minimalist graphic design. The absence of color should heighten form, mood, and composition.

Contrast can be high and dramatic or soft and misty, but it must be controlled. Avoid accidental flat gray. Texture should help distinguish materials even without color.

### Color Vocabulary

The palette includes pure black, charcoal, dark gray, mid gray, silver gray, pearl gray, off-white, paper white, and sometimes a single tinted monochrome such as sepia, blue-gray, or warm gray.

The image should depend on value hierarchy rather than hue variety.

### Ready-to-use Prompt

Monochrome style, black-and-white or single-tone palette, strong value structure, deliberate highlights, midtones and shadows, clear silhouettes, controlled contrast, grayscale texture, ink, charcoal, film grain or paper surface, composition driven by light and dark rather than color, pure black, charcoal, mid gray, silver gray, pearl gray and off-white tones, dramatic or quiet tonal atmosphere, refined monochrome visual language.

### Negative Prompt

Avoid full color, random saturated accents, muddy uncontrolled gray, weak value contrast, plastic CGI color lighting, neon palette, flat color blocks without tonal depth, messy unreadable shadows, no texture, no light structure, oversaturated background.
