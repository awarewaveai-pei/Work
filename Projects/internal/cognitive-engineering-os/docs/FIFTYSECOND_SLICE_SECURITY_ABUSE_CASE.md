**The core real invariant is grounded DIRECTLY in Atlas's own citation** (a stronger basis than DPIA/PIA's own two invariants, which are disclosed as real external GDPR practice, NOT Atlas): Atlas's own five-word chain names `controls` and `detection` as TWO SEPARATE, DISTINCT final elements, not one merged "mitigation" step. This contract therefore requires, for EVERY step declared in the `abuse_flow` stage: (a) at least one `controls` entry addressing it, AND (b) INDEPENDENTLY, at least one `detection` entry addressing it -- both directions (forward referential validity and reverse completeness) checked for both arrays. A step with a control but zero detection (or vice versa) is an incomplete abuse case under this schema.

**Real usage precedent, drawn from an already-documented threat, not invented from scratch (stronger due diligence than PASTA's own fabricated example)**: `docs/architecture/SECURITY_THREAT_MODEL.md` (this repository's own real STRIDE+LINDDUN threat review) names two real scenarios this slice's own CLI demo models directly, landing on OPPOSITE sides of the core invariant:

Sigstore/cosign keyless signing (Sub-decision (b), no long-lived key exists to steal) is the control; Rekor's public append-only transparency log (named explicitly in SECURITY_THREAT_MODEL.md's own T1 row: "OIDC 短期憑證 + Fulcio + Rekor 透明日誌") is the detection mechanism. This abuse case **validates** cleanly.
2. **T2/E4/Uw1 (Untrusted Content → AI 決策流 → Knowledge Memory) / Prioritized Threat List item 6** -- a real, STILL-OPEN gap.
